# Apollo 11 (`/apo/`)

The real command module Columbia, from the Smithsonian's CC0 3D scans (interior hull, couches, panels and graffiti layer, plus the exterior shell), at real size in lunar orbit. Interior and exterior scans share one frame but for a fitted 9.5 cm offset. Columbia is tipped so the crew's line of sight to the main panel is level, so a standing visitor faces it as the crew did, and turned so its heat shield faces the low morning Sun. The Moon (LRO colour and LOLA elevation from NASA's CGI Moon Kit, slope-shaded in a shader with a sharp 80 by 40 degree patch around the Sea of Tranquility) and Earth (July Blue Marble with clouds) are scaled 1:1000 so every angle stays true; Earth hangs where the hatch window looks. Tour stops sit on the curators' annotation points. There is no floor in orbit: in the headset, the left thumbstick floats you where you look and the right one turns and raises or lowers you (`src/float.js`); flat screens turn in place inside. `scripts/prepare-apollo.py` downloads the scans and builds the textures; sources are in `apps/apo/public/models/CREDITS.md`. Visitors here wear Neil Armstrong's scanned A7-L suit and helmet, gold visor down (`scripts/prepare-apollo-suit.py`, run with Blender): `createPresence`'s `outfit: { suit, helmet }` puts instances of them on every avatar, so all suits are one draw call and all helmets another. The helmet turns with where someone looks; with faces hidden, the name tag lighting up shows who's talking. That scan is not CC0 (Smithsonian non-commercial educational terms), so this room must stay free unless the Smithsonian agrees otherwise. For the same reason `suit.glb` and `helmet.glb` are not in the public repository: without them visitors appear as the plain avatar, and `scripts/prepare-apollo-suit.py` builds them from the Smithsonian download.
