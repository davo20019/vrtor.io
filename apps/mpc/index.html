About this experience

Six separate laser-survey areas, with measured stone geometry and gaps in coverage. Not a continuous model of the entire citadel. The mountain backdrop uses elevation data around Machu Picchu. Placement, local ground and sky are approximate and illustrative. Original grayscale vertex values are preserved. The scans have no photographic surface textures; granite colors, procedural mineral grain and lighting are illustrative.

Credit: Instituto Nacional de Cultura (Peru), Center for Advanced Spatial Technologies (University of Arkansas), and Cotsen Institute for Archaeology (UCLA).

Data acquired and processed by CAST staff Snow Winters, Malcolm Williamson and Katie Simon, and students in the 2009 UCLA Cuzco/Machu Picchu Field School, directed by Alexei Vranich. Developed under the authority of INC, Vladimir Dávila and Fernando Astete.

Survey source · CC BY-NC 3.0 · Download credits and changes. Units, origin, normals, materials and compression adapted by vrtor. No additional mesh reduction.

Terrain: Mapzen Terrain Tiles, SRTM/GMTED2010 data courtesy of the U.S. Geological Survey. Heights resampled; the local join and surface colors are illustrative.

This educational experience is free, without ads, subscriptions or paid-product promotion.

Learning sources: UNESCO and the Peruvian Ministry of Culture’s research publication.
