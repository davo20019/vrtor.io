What the other suggested sources hold (checked):
- Petrie Pl. xii has **no** gallery-hole layout (it shows casing details, the two dressed-out bosses of the King's Chamber, the antechamber wainscot hollows, Third Pyramid panel, Abu Roash statue). Pl. ix is the 1:500 section only. Pl. xiii is the "errors" diagram of the four King's Chamber walls (course lines, ceiling and floor deviations, exaggerated); it draws **no vertical joints**. Petrie §46 gives only the long/short hole means and §52 only the top-course joints.
- Edgar, *Great Pyramid Passages* vol. 1 (1910) and vol. 2 (1913) (archive.org `GreatPyramidPassagesVol11910Edition`, `GreatPyramidPassagesVol21913Edition`): full text searched; no ramp-hole table and no King's Chamber joint measurements. - Maragioglio & Rinaldi and Dormion 2004: not public, not consulted.

Cross-check that settles which source is primary for the holes: computed from Smyth's table below, the 14 long holes on each side average 23.33 in (E) and 23.32 in (W), and the 28 short holes average 20.51 in. Those are exactly Petrie's §46 figures (long 23.32, short 20.51), so **Petrie's means were computed from Smyth's table**; Smyth is the only primary hole survey.

## 1. Grand Gallery ramp holes (slots)

### 1.1 How Smyth measured them (S pp. 78-79, 75)

- "These holes are cut in the ramps, **next the wall**, rather rudely, and have their edges now much broken" (S p. 78). Plate 9 of vol. 2 (plan of the S end) draws them as black rectangles against the wall face of each ramp. So each slot sits on the wall side of the ramp, its long axis along the gallery.
- "Their upper and lower, or north and south sides, are cut **nearly vertical**, certainly far from at right angles to the general incline" (S p. 78). Model the N and S ends as vertical planes.
- Depth "(vertical) varies from eight to eleven inches, probably as influenced by hardened dirt" (S p. 78). A "+" before a depth in his table means "at least" (dirt at the bottom).
- 28 per side **including** one in the SE and one in the SW inner corner of the upper surface of the great step (S p. 78). "The holes on either ramp are always opposite, or nearly so" (S p. 79).
- Distances are along the inclined ramp top from the N wall of the gallery (S p. 77: "measured along surface of ramps"); for hole 28 they continue along the ramp line produced over the step to the S wall. Measures "only taken once, excepting a few checks" (S p. 79).
- Smyth's whole length on that line: E 1882.6, W 1883.0 in (S p. 78); Petrie's continuous tape on the E ramp 1883.6 in (P §45). Difference 0.05 %, ignored below.

### 1.2 Derivation used for the frame columns (derived)

- θ = 26°16'40" (P §45 mean axis). z = 41.321 - s·cos θ, with s the sloping distance in metres. Check: Smyth's ramp end at the step (E 1813.9, W 1815.6 in) maps to z = +0.009 / -0.030, against the step face at z = -0.010 (P §46); his S wall (1882.6 / 1883.0 in) maps to z = -1.555 / -1.565, against -1.567. So the projection holds to 2-4 cm.
- "ramp-top y at mid" = 21.656 + 0.606 + s_mid·sin θ, i.e. your floor line at the N wall plus your 0.606 m ramp height taken as vertical. Smyth's own ramp figures: vertical height 23.0 in, breadth 20.0 in (S p. 72); per-station breadth E mean 20.14, W 20.04 in; height **at right angles to the incline** E 21.03, W 20.93 in (S p. 83). 21.0 in perpendicular = 23.4 in vertical, so your 0.606 m (23.86 in, P §30) is consistent. Treat the y column as derived.
- Horizontal length of a slot = L_slope · cos θ (because the ends are vertical). Long holes: 23.32 × cos θ = 20.91 in = 0.531 m; short holes 20.51 × cos θ = 18.39 in = 0.467 m (derived).
- Hole 28 (on the step, horizontal surface): its S side is the S wall (S p. 74-75: the "reappearance of ramp joint" runs to the S wall; S p. 75 for the SW hole). zS set to -1.567, zN = zS + L·cos θ (derived; its horizontal length is uncertain by about 2 in because Smyth's figure is on the produced ramp line).
- Breadth (E-W, across the ramp, measured from the wall face): column "breadth E-W". So each slot spans x from the wall face to wall face ∓ breadth (E side: x from x_Ewall - b to x_Ewall; W side: x_Wwall to x_Wwall + b).

### 1.4 Stones inserted in the walls above the holes ("niches")

- "Of these, all, except the two on the great step, and the two lowest or northernmost on either side (i.e., four at the north end) have a piece of stone let into the wall **vertically over their middle**; the height of such inserted piece being usually **18"**, and breadth **13"**; while the depth or thickness in one particular case where a neighbouring fracture enables it to be seen, is about **10"**" (S pp. 78-79). So inserted stones over holes 3 to 27 on each side = **25 per side, 50 in all**, which matches the commonly cited 25. Column "inserted stone above" marks them.
- Their dimensions in metres: 0.457 high × 0.330 wide (along the gallery) × about 0.254 deep into the wall. Centre along the gallery: the hole's mid-point (z mid column). S p. 75 calls them "inserted vertically, or nearly so".
- Height of the inserted stone's bottom above the ramp top: **not measured** by Smyth or Petrie. Petrie §46 adds only that a rough groove is chopped across each inserted block ("the rough chopping out of a groove across each block"), with no dimensions.
- Many of the gallery's lowest wall joints are "absorbed" in the vertical joints of these stones (S p. 75), see 1.6.

## 2. King's Chamber wall joints

### 2.1 Source and method

Smyth vol. 2 p. 108: "Each of the above five courses is formed in the run of its length, of many blocks ... I measured the lengths, however, in the three lower courses; and am enabled now, by the kindness of Mr. Aiton, to add the measures of all five, as taken soon after I had left by Mr. Aiton's assistant, Mr. Inglis." He adds that the measures "are very rude, and not worthy of competing in their united lengths for determining the whole length of any of the walls". Tables "East/West/North/South Wall, vertical Joints in" (S pp. 108-109) give each joint's distance from a stated wall end. Smyth's Plate 13 draws all four walls opened out from these numbers. Courses 1-3: Smyth's own measure (Aiton & Inglis as a check, mostly within 0.5 in); courses 4-5: Aiton & Inglis only.

Course heights (P §52): mean course 47.040 ± 0.013 in = 1.1948 m; average variation 0.051 in. Lowest course goes 5 in below the floor (S p. 106: seen through the hole in the NW corner of the floor; first course is 42.0-42.7 in above the floor in his table on p. 107). Course joint y (derived, from your wall base 42.888): 44.083, 45.278, 46.472, 47.667; top of course 5 = 48.862 (ceiling 48.809-48.862, P §55). Petrie Pl. xiii shows the first course line rising 1.03 in from NW to NE and dropping along the S wall (P §51); the upper course lines were connected only in the corners.

Frame columns (derived): "unscaled" uses Smyth's inches directly from the stated end; "scaled" stretches each course so its printed total equals your frame wall length (N/S 10.486 m, E/W 5.243 m). Use "scaled" so the last joint never lands outside the wall.

Notes on single rows (course 5 rows of the N and S tables below are Inglis's and are superseded by Petrie, see 2.2):
- N wall, course 1: the last span (372.1 to 413.3 in from W, 41.2 in) is the **doorway**, not a block (Smyth labels joint 7 "Door side"). It runs x = 6.705 to 7.750 (scaled).
- N wall, course 2: the 122.7 in block from 289.9 in to the E end is the "immense block of granite over the doorway" with the diagonal drafted line (P §55). Course 3 prints the same 122.7 in at the same end; possibly a repeat, Smyth does not comment.
- N wall, course 3: joints 1-2 bracketed by Smyth (his 36.0/63.6 against Inglis's 48.5/51.4): "a case of certain blunder, either of mine, or Mr. Inglis's". Low confidence for those two.
- E wall, course 3: joints 2-3 likewise bracketed (Smyth 44.8/58.2 against Inglis 33.0/69.0). Low confidence.
- S wall, course 1: joint 8 prints total 351.4 but 310.8 + 40.3 = 351.1; joint 9 prints 393.7 but 351.4 + 42.6 = 394.0. Printed totals used (last total 412.8 closes).
- N wall, course 1: joint 6 prints 310.0, sum gives 310.1.
