## 0. Tying SP23's frame to Petrie's (the key conversion)

SP23 publish a side profile and a front view of the chevron area drawn from the laser/photogrammetry model made by
Cairo University, HIP and Emissive (SP23 Methods, Fig. 5a/5b; same profile in Fig. 3d). Their frame (SP23 Methods,
Table 1 note): origin x = north face of the Chevron, y = E-W centre of the Chevron, z = "the lower cross point of the
huge gabled limestone beams" (the apex of the lower pair's underside); x positive north in the figure, **y positive
west**, z up.

Digitized from SP23 Fig. 5b (full-size PNG, 1349x643; axis ticks at 16.47 px/m in x, 16.5 px/m in z, reading +/-0.06 m):
- Descending corridor (DC) floor line: z = -8.272 + 0.4947 x (26.32 deg; Petrie 26.52 deg). Roof 1.33 m above it
  vertically (Petrie: 1.200/cos 26.52 = 1.341).
- Ascending corridor (AC) floor line: z = -14.52 - 0.5046 (x + 16.33).
- Their intersection (= Petrie's floor junction, P §38/§64: z = 76.614, y = 4.569): **x = -14.50, z = -15.44**.

So **zP = xSP + 91.11** and **yP = zSP + 20.01**. A second tie, Petrie's DC floor line evaluated at xSP = 0
(zP 91.11: yP = 16.972 - 0.49897 x (101.854 - 91.11) = 11.611) against the model floor (z = -8.272), gives
yP = zSP + 19.88. **Adopted: yP = zSP + 19.95 +/- 0.07, zP = xSP + 91.1 +/- 0.3.**

Independent checks of this tie:
- MR Tav. 2 Fig. 10 (median section, levels marked in Petrie's course levels) puts the bottom of the lower chevron's
  butment at **19.93 m** (digitized, see 1.2). The tie gives 19.95.
- The core face above the chevron (SP23 Fig. 5b line through (0, 8.2) and (-5.04, 14.88), 53 deg) converts to
  zP 91.11 at yP 28.15 and zP 86.07 at yP 34.83. Casing line at those heights, (146.71 - y)/tan 51 deg 52':
  93.13 and 87.82. So the core stands **2.0 m and 1.75 m** behind the casing line, matching the ~1.8 m mid-face offset.
- The DC roof mouth (first surviving roof block) converts to zP 94.71, yP 14.85; MR Fig. 10 gives zP 94.84, yP 15.16
  (digitized, tied to the doorway at the casing). Perring (V42 p. 110: 63 ft 2 in from the beginning of the roof to the
  junction) gives zP 93.83.
- SP23 Table 1: Nagoya NFC centre Y = +0.03 m (west of the chevron centre), CEA: NFC is -7 +/- 4 cm from the DC axis;
  Fig. 5a shows the DC centred at y = +0.01. MR Fig. 8 puts the butment 3 cm west of the DC centre (digitized).
  **Chevron centre line: xP = 7.29 +/- 0.03** (DC mid-line at the doorway, P §35). Conversion: **xP = 7.29 - ySP**.

## 1. The chevrons (two remaining pairs of gabled limestone beams)

### 1.1 What the sources say (read)

- Two pairs "laid one upon the other", "on an almost vertical plane" (MR p. 26, English column); MR Tav. 2 Fig. 10
  draws both faces in one vertical plane. SP23 Fig. 5b draws them as one slab, front face at x = 0 from z = 0 to 5.64,
  joint at z = 2.67. **So the upper pair is flush with the lower pair**; Petrie's measured leans of face are small:
  upper E 20' to 2 deg in, upper W 2 deg 20' in, lower E 20'-30' out, lower W 25'-30' in (P §34). Over the upper pair's
  2.9 m that is at most 0.12 m set-back at its top (derived).
- Thickness (N-S): **33 in = 0.838 m** (P §34); "about 0.84 m" (MR p. 27); NFC begins 0.84 +/- 0.05 m behind the
  chevron's north face, "about the same thickness" (SP23). No other blocks directly behind them (P §34).
- Angle on the butment, mean of 12: **50 deg 28' +/- 5'** (P §34), so the beams rise at **39 deg 32'** from horizontal
  (derived, 90 deg - 50 deg 28'). SP23 Fig. 5a model edges: 40.5-41 deg (digitized).
- Block sizes, P §34 table (in; m), MR Tav. 2 Fig. 8 repeats them in metres (X = upper E, Y = upper W, W = lower E,
  Z = lower W, as seen from the north, east on the left):

  Brackets: deduced by Petrie. Vyse/Perring (V42 p. 110 note 7): lower blocks **7 ft (2.13 m)** wide, upper
  **6 ft 8 in (2.03 m)**; the block immediately over the entrance 12 ft 6 in (3.81 m) wide and 8 ft 6 in (2.59 m) high

- Gone pairs in front (P §34, MR p. 27): the next pair's top was "some 70 inches lower" (1.78 m; MR Fig. 10 writes

## 2. Al-Ma'mun's tunnel (the robbers' tunnel, today's tourist entrance)

### 2.1 Read

- Mouth: "It was in the centre, of considerable size" (V40 p. 213, 6 April 1837, when it was uncovered). SP23 Intro:
  the passage "dug by al-Ma'mun ... from the central axis of the pyramid to the drop stone located at the intersection"
  of the DC and AC.
- Level: "The breach opens lower down than the original entrance, that is above the 7th course. The almost horizontal
  passage leading from it reaches the ascending corridor (A) at the level of the granite plugs" (MR p. 24, English).
  Italian: "si apre sopra il 7° corso". "Now enlarged and heightened to make passage easy" (MR p. 24 Italian:
  "ora allargata ed innalzata"). Tyldesley 2007 pp. 38-40 (via W): forced into the 6th and 7th courses, about 7 m up.
- Length: about 27 m straight, then a sharp turn left (east) (Tyldesley via W). Greaves (1646), reprinted in V42
  p. 209: "The forced passage ... extended eighty-nine feet" (27.1 m), "of various heights". Greaves came in by the
  original sloping doorway (V42 pp. 208-209), and the tunnel's mouth was then buried in rubbish (V42 p. 222 note, of a
  1674 visit), so which stretch his 89 ft measures is not certain.
- Plan: "Hearing the stone drop, they turned aside their southward progress, and burrowing some twenty feet eastwards
  they broke into the entrance passage"; then "a continuation southward and upward to get behind the granite plug";
  they "tracked along them in the softer limestone, until they reached the upper end" (P §177). "The Arabs, on forcing
  the way by Mamun's Hole, ran along the side of the granite plugs" (P §125). The tunnel round the
  plugs is on the **west** side: "A short way down from this point [the roof junction] in the west corridor wall is the
  opening of the so called Ma'amun's Hole and here the second and third granite plugs are visible. The hole joins up
  with the tunnel made by violators" (MR p. 27-28).
- Perring's table (V42 p. 110): along the DC, from the junction at the bottom of the upper passage "thence to the
  forced passage 17 ft 10 in" (5.44 m).
- Petrie Pl. IX labels "MAMUN'S FORCED PASSAGE" in the band of course 7 (between his drawn course lines for 7.06 and
  8.06, which match `petrie_courses.csv` at 22.85 px/m), and draws "MAMUN'S HOLE" as a dotted cavity round the plugs.
  Petrie tied his survey through the hole (P §31, §39) but gives no dimensions.
- SP23: emulsion detectors EM5, EM6H, EM6T, EM7 "in three narrow hollows on the wall of the MC" (Results); Joliot on "a
  dedicated wooden platform built at the intersection between the DC and the AC" (CEA Results).
  **Caution:** SP23's "~10 m from behind the Chevron" and "1.5-2.0 m in height and 2.0-2.5 m in width" in the MC
  section describe the **NFC as seen from the MC detectors**, not the tunnel.

### 2.3 Cross-section

**Not found as a measured number.** MR: "enlarged and heightened" for visitors. Greaves (1646): "of various heights".
SP23 Fig. 1 render (scaled by the chevron, low confidence): mouth about 2.7 m wide and 3 m high, irregular, arched.
The ScanPyramids 2023 detectors fit in "narrow hollows" in its walls (SP23).

**Confidence (tunnel):** x of the mouth on the axis: high (two read sources plus the render). Straight length 27 m:
medium (single secondary figure, may come from Greaves). Floor level: low (MR and Tyldesley disagree by one course,
6.1 vs 8.06). Bend: medium for direction and ~6 m (Petrie), low for exact z. Hole envelope round the plugs: medium
(Petrie's 1/500 plate, +/-0.3 m). Section: unknown.
