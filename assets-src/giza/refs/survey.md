# Great Pyramid of Giza (Khufu, G1): published survey measurements

Compiled 2026-10-26 for an exact-scale replica. Every number cites its source. Petrie's inches are kept and converted at 1 in = 0.0254 m exactly (Petrie used British imperial inches; the difference from the 1959 international inch is about 2 parts per million, below his precision).

## Coordinate frame (use this to place everything)

Petrie's own frame (P §35 note 1; §64):
- **Origin:** centre of the base square of the casing, at **pavement level** (Petrie's zero of levels is "a considerable flat-dressed surface of rock at the N.E. corner, which is evidently intended to be at the level of the pavement", P §23 [p. 41]).
- **N/S:** distance from a vertical E-W plane through the centre, parallel to the mean of the N and S sides. **E/W:** likewise from the N-S plane parallel to the mean of the E and W sides (P §35 note 1).
- Base square side 9068.8 in, so each face's foot is 4534.4 in (115.174 m) from the centre. "From N base" + "N of centre" = 4534.4 in.
- Petrie's axes are the pyramid's own axes, which are rotated about -3' 43" from true north (P §21). Dash 2015 gives -3' 54" ± 44" for the meridian (D15 p. 11).

Recommended replica frame: x = east, y = north, z = up, metres, origin at the centre of the base at pavement level, axes along the pyramid's sides (not true north).

### 1.3 Present top (in Petrie's day, 1880-82)

- Top of course 201 (the platform course): N.E. 5407.9 in (137.361 m), S.W. 5409.2 in (137.394 m) (P-Pl.8; quoted in P §25 [p. 43]).
- Remnants of courses 202 and 203 survived, measurable only at the N.E.: tops 5430.4 in (137.932 m) and 5451.8 in (138.476 m) (P-Pl.8, Birdsall's note on courses 202-203).
- Top platform size: at the level of course 201, the mean planes of the core faces measured from the centre of the base horizontally: N side 224.5 ± 0.7, E side 214.1 ± 0.3, S side 215.0 ± 0.4, W side 217.6 ± 1.0 in (P §25 [p. 43], mean of 1881 and 1882). So the core platform is **N-S 439.5 in (11.163 m) by E-W 431.7 in (10.965 m)**, off-centre 4.75 in to the N and 1.75 in to the W (derived here from P §25). Petrie: "The top is, rather strangely, not square".
- The 1874 Transit of Venus staff stood on the top and its place is known within ± ½ in (P §25). (A modern wooden survey mast stands there today; its size was not found.)
- Casing thickness implied at that level: 60.8 ± 3 in on N, 86.6 in on S, 77.6 mean E and W (P §25).

### 1.4 Courses: Petrie Plate viii (full table)

**File:** `petrie_courses.csv` (in this folder), 203 rows, one per course.

**Where it came from:** Petrie's handwritten Plate viii, "Levels of the Courses of the Great Pyramid", as transcribed by Ronald Birdsall, "Corrected Great Pyramid Course Elevations" (2003), who compared two original copies of the 1883 book (P-Pl.8, Wayback URL above). The table was parsed from Birdsall's HTML by script; every row was checked: NE level(n) - NE level(n-1) = NE thickness(n) holds for all 203 courses (0 mismatches).

Columns (all inches except `_m`):
- `ne_top_in`: level of the **top** of the course at the N.E. corner above the pavement (Petrie levelled up the N.E. and S.W. corners, P §23).
- `ne_thick_in`: course height at N.E. (= difference of levels).
- `sw_top_in`, `sw_thick_in`: the same at the S.W. corner (none for 202, 203).
- `var_level_in`: difference N.E. vs S.W. level. `var_thick_in`: difference of thickness N.E. vs S.W. `var_thick_4corners_in`: range of thickness over all four corners, **estimated by Birdsall from Petrie's bar graphs** (less reliable).
- `ne_top_m`, `ne_thick_m`: N.E. values in metres.
- `annotated`: Y where Birdsall corrected or annotated Petrie:
  - Course 2, S.W.: Petrie gave 110.0 and 110.2 (one includes the 0.2 in closing error of the levelling circuit that Petrie put "all together at the junction of levelling at the 2nd course of the S.W.", P §23); Birdsall entered the average, 110.1.
  - Course 3: thickness bars on the plate disagree with the computed S.W. thickness.
  - **Course 70: Petrie's plate reads 2337.7 / 2337.9, an obvious slip; corrected to 2237.7 / 2237.9.**
  - Course 110: thickness bars misplaced (22.x for 23.x); levels unaffected.
  - Course 132, S.W.: 3888.9 (secondary sources often give 3888.0; Birdsall reads a faint 9 on magnification).
  - Courses 202-203: only N.E. measurable in Petrie's time.

Key facts from the table and text:
- **203 courses** from pavement to the remnant top in 1880-82 (201 complete, 202-203 fragments at N.E.). Petrie numbers the first course from the pavement; Piazzi Smyth's numbers are one higher, and his levels about 20 in too high, because he took the pavement's rock bed for the pavement (P §23 note 1).
- Course 1: 58.6 in at N.E. (1.488 m), 57.6 in at S.W. The tallest course. At the N casing stones course 1 tops out at +58.85 in (P §26 [p. 44]).
- Thinnest courses: 19.7-20 in (about 0.50-0.51 m), near courses 154-187.
- Course 19 (the entrance course): 37.94 ± 0.17 in, "a sudden increase of thickness, none being so large for 11 courses before it and 14 after it" (P §32 [p. 52]); levels of the 19th course by the entrance 668.30 and 705.97 in (P §32). Courses 20-21 are unusually thin (23.5, 23.8 in): two courses combined for a thick lintel (P §32).
- Course 35 (49.8 in at N.E., 1.265 m) is the tall course often noted part way up.
- Petrie's levels were read to 1/100 in but published to 1/10 in (P §23).

**Confidence:** high for the N.E. and S.W. corner levels (Petrie's spirit-levelling, closing error 0.2 in over the full circuit, P §23). These are corner levels: courses dip and vary across the faces ("the courses of the core had dips of as much as ½° to 1°", P §24 [p. 42]), and the `var_thick_4corners_in` column is read off a graph. Courses 202-203 survive only as fragments.

Remaining casing courses: the casing survives only at the foot of the N side, in course 1. Dash: of the 920 m periphery, only 54 m of casing remains in place, much of it damaged (D15 p. 8). The core courses are assumed to follow the casing courses (P §32).

### 1.5 Surviving casing stones (N side, base)

- Height = course 1: front edge +58.83 to +58.90 in above pavement (W end, middle, E end), mean plane +58.85 in (1.495 m), varying only 0.02 (P §26 [p. 44]).
- Thickness: top 62 ± 8 in (1.575 m), bottom 108 ± 8 in (2.743 m) (P §26 [p. 43]).
- Joint widths: mean 0.020 in (0.5 mm), range 0.002-0.045 in; joints cemented; joint area about 35 sq ft; stone weight "some 16 tons" (P §26 [p. 44]).
- Length up the face of the joints measured: 75 in (P §26).
- One of the three in-situ stones is 0.9 in proud of the next at the top while flush at the base (P §24).
- Casing thickness at the corners averages only 33.7 in (difference of core plane and casing on the pavement) because the core faces are hollowed (P §26).

### 1.6 Concavity of the core faces ("indentation")

"This hollowing is a striking feature; and beside the general curve of the face, each side has a sort of groove specially down the middle of the face". Whole hollowing estimated at **37 in (0.94 m) on the N face**; casing thickness 33.7 in at the corners plus 37 gives 70.7 in at the mid-line (P §26 [p. 44]). Petrie gives no figure for the other faces. The core is also twisted relative to the base by a mean 1' 33" (P §22 [p. 41]).

### 1.7 Pavement, rock bed and sockets

- Pavement (limestone) thickness: **17 to 27 in (0.43-0.69 m)**; 21 in (0.533 m) where Vyse found it (P §27 [p. 46]). Levels of the pavement's rock bed: -15.9 to -27.1 in below pavement top (P §27 [p. 45]).
- Maximum remaining width on N side when found by Vyse: 402 in (10.21 m) (P §27).
- Edge of the rock-cut bed of the pavement, measured from the casing foot: 529.0 to 627.9 in (13.44-15.95 m); irregular, "varying 99 inches"; "if [the fine paving was regular] it cannot have exceeded 529 in width" (P §27 [p. 45]). Individual values: N.N.W. 616.9; N side 615.9, 618.7, 616.2; opposite entrance 564-568; N.E. corner 529.0 (N), 538.8 (E); E.N.E. 533.9; S.S.W. 536.5, 533.0, 534.6; S.W. corner 529.6 (S), 536.0 (W); W.S.W. 627.9 in.
- Dash: platform projects 0.39-0.47 m beyond the casing foot (D15 p. 8). Note this is the platform under the casing, which Petrie calls the pavement; Petrie's 529-628 in is the outer edge of its rock-cut bed.
- **Corner sockets**, floor level below pavement (P §22 [p. 40], "accurate in 1882"): N.E. -28.5 in (-0.724 m), S.E. -39.9 (-1.013 m), S.W. -23.0 (-0.584 m), N.W. -32.8 (-0.833 m). The casing was continued down into the sockets, so the socket edges stand 20-30 in outside the base at pavement level; the projected casing falls on average 4 in inside the socket edges (P §20).
- Basalt pavement (E side, temple floor): + 2.0 in above the limestone pavement (P §27). Plan: two squares of about 1,060 in (26.9 m) side; E edge 2153.0 (N end) and 2148.0 (S end) in from the pyramid base (P §28 [p. 46-47]). Dash: the Pyramid Temple doorway is 52.5 m from the E casing, 168 m E of the centre (D15 p. 11). W: Pyramid Temple 52.2 m N-S by 40 m E-W (W, citing Arnold 2005).

Levels round the base (P §23 [p. 41], zero = pavement): 1st course top N.E. +58.6, S.W. +57.6, N.W. +58.0, N +58.9; 2nd course top N.E. +107.7, S.E. +105.5, S.W. +111.2, N.W. +106.6, N +107.4 in.

## 2. Entrance (north face)

- **Original doorway** (floor at the casing face): **+668.2 ± 0.1 in (16.972 m) above pavement; 524.1 ± 0.3 in (13.312 m) S of the N casing edge; middle 287.0 ± 0.8 in (7.290 m) E of the centre**; i.e. 3723.6 in from the E side and 4297.6 in from the W side at that level (P §35 [p. 55]).
- It occupies the whole height of course 19: 37.94 ± 0.17 in vertically (P §32). W (citing Maragioglio & Rinaldi): course 19 is 96 cm high, the entrance about 17 m above the base and 15 cubits E of the centre line.
- Data used by Petrie (P §32 [p. 52]): station mark on the passage floor at level 611.2 in, 638.4 in inside the casing base line; passage angle at mouth 26° 29' ± 1'; passage height 47.26 in.
- End of the built passage where it meets the rock: E roof 1350.7 in down the slope from the original entrance (P §35).

### 2.2 Robbers' tunnel ("al-Ma'mun's hole"), the tourist entrance

- Forced into the 6th and 7th courses, **about 7 m above the base**; runs roughly straight and horizontal for **27 m**, then turns sharply left (east) to meet the granite plugs of the ascending passage (W, citing Tyldesley 2007 pp. 38-40). Check: Petrie's course 6 top is 278.1 in (7.064 m) at N.E. (P-Pl.8).
- A short tunnel was cut round the plugs to reach the descending passage (W, citing Dormion 2004 p. 286).
- Petrie calls it "Mamun's Hole" and tied his survey through it (P §31, §39) but gives **no dimensions** for it. SP23 calls it the "al-Ma'mun Corridor (MC)".
- **Gap:** no measured plan or section of the tunnel (cross-section, exact E offset, floor level) was found. Its mouth is W of the original entrance's axis (photo: Commons "Cheops-Pyramid@Eingaenge.JPG").

## 3. Descending (entrance) passage and the subterranean parts

- **Angle:** mean axis of the whole length 26° 31' 23" ± 5"; built part alone 26° 26' 42" ± 20" (P §36 [p. 58]). At the junction with the ascending passage 26° 21' (Smyth, used by P §38). W (citing Dormion 2004 p. 284): 26° 26' 46" (1:2).

- **Length:** S end of the floor is 4140 in (105.156 m) along the slope from the original entrance (P §36 [p. 59]). Its S end is at level **-1181 ± 1 in (-29.997 m)**, 4228 ± 2 in (107.391 m) from the N base = 306 in (7.772 m) N of the centre, 286.4 ± 1.0 in (7.275 m) E of centre (P §36).

- Points along the floor from the original entrance (P §35 [p. 57], rough measure ± 2-3 in): fissure fillings 1,555-1,629; scaled sides 3,066-3,116; **mouth of the passage to the gallery (well shaft) 3,825-3,856 (W side)**; end of sloping roof 4,143 (E).
- **Where the ascending passage leaves:** the ascending passage floor cuts the descending passage floor at **1110.64 in (28.210 m)** along the slope from the casing face; its roof starts vertically over 1110.90 (P §38 [p. 61-62]). That point is at +179.9 in (4.569 m) above pavement, 1517.8 in (38.552 m) from the N base (P §64). W: after 28 m from the entrance.

## 4. Ascending passage

- **Length (on the slope):** 1546.8 in (39.289 m) from the floor intersection with the descending passage to the floor joint at the gallery doorway; horizontal 1389.5 in (35.293 m), vertical 679.7 in (17.264 m) (P §38 [p. 63], §39 [p. 65]). W (Dormion): 75 cubits.
- **Angle:** mean 26° 2' 30" (P §38-39); the plugged lower part 26° 12½' (P §38 [p. 61]); combined passage + gallery 26° 12' 50" (P §39). W (Dormion p. 286): 26° 6'.
- **Azimuth:** -4' ± 3' (P §39).
- **Width:** 41.6 in (1.057 m) at the upper end; contracted to 38.2 in (0.970 m) at the lower (N) end to hold the plugs. **Height** unchanged: 47.30 E, 47.15 mid, 47.26 W at the lower end; 47.3 in (1.201 m) at the upper end (P §38 [p. 63]).
- **Granite plugs:** red granite, cut from boulders. Lower end of the plugs 74.19 in from the floor intersection; present top 252.7 in; original top about 277 in (the top block is broken; Petrie estimated 24 in more originally) (P §38). So the plug train is about **178.5 in (4.53 m) now, about 203 in (5.15 m) originally** (derived). Count and lengths (W, citing Dormion 2004 p. 286): **3 blocks, 1.57, 1.67 and 1.0 m** (top one damaged). The 2nd block from the top jammed 4 in above the 3rd (P §38).
- **Girdle stones:** blocks wholly or partly encircling the passage, at intervals of 10 cubits (206.3-208.9 in = 5.24-5.31 m) along the slope (P §38 [p. 64], first noted by Waynman Dixon). W (citing Maragioglio & Rinaldi pp. 114-115): three girdle stones near the middle; joints in the lower third are vertical.
- Two horizontal roof joints S of the plugs, 60.5 (60.1) in apart vertically, are not a hidden passage (P §38).
- End: floor at the latitude of the gallery E wall is at +852.6 ± 0.3 in (21.656 m), 2907.3 in (73.845 m) from the N base = 1626.8 ± 0.8 in (41.321 m) N of centre, mid-line 287 ± 1.5 in E (P §39 [p. 65]).

## 5. Horizontal passage and Queen's Chamber

## 6. Grand Gallery (P §45-46)

- **Length on the slope** (E ramp, 6 in from its edge): N wall 0; ramp end 1815.5 in (46.114 m); S wall in the same line 1883.6 in (47.843 m) (P §45 [p. 71]). Roof length 1648.4 in horizontal (41.869 m) = 1838.6 in sloping (46.700 m) (P §46 [p. 74]). W: 46.68 m long.
- **Angle:** mean axis altitude 26° 16' 40", azimuth +1' 20" (P §45 table header).
- **Floor (between ramps):** 2 cubits; gallery width between walls at floor 82.42 in (2.093 m) (P §30, mean diff 0.44). W (Maragioglio & Rinaldi): base 4 cubits.
- **Ramps (benches):** about 1 cubit wide each (W, citing Lehner 1997 p. 113); ramp height 23.86 in (0.606 m), mean diff 0.32 (P §30). Space between ramps = 2 cubits = width at the top (P §46).
- **Height:** lower edge of the groove in the 3rd lap is at half the gallery's height, "that varying from 167 to 172" in, i.e. **about 334-344 in (8.48-8.74 m) vertically** (P §46 [p. 74], using Smyth's 166.2 ± 0.8 in for the 3rd lap). W: 8.6 m high.
- **Corbels:** 7 laps (overlaps) on each side above the walls, each about 3 in ("one palm"), total overhang 20.55 in (0.522 m) = one cubit (P §46 [p. 74]). Laps begin above the 2nd course at 2.29 m (W, citing Maragioglio & Rinaldi). Petrie measured lap heights only at the ends (table in §46).
- **Roof width:** 40.9 in (1.039 m) at N, 41.3 in (1.049 m) at S (P §46 [p. 73]).
- **Roof slabs:** each set steeper than the floor (28° 0'-28° 30', mean 28° 11') and hooked into ratchet cuts 1.0-2.0 in deep in the wall tops; slab 52.2 in N-S typical, next 47.4; the southernmost slab edge is 14.5 in from the S wall (P §46 [p. 72-73]).
- **Groove** along the 3rd lap, full length: from 11.4 to 5.3 in above the lap edge (mean), cut 0.6-0.8 in deep (P §46).
- **Ramp holes (slots):** alternately long and short; long holes mean 23.32 in (0.592 m), short 20.51 in (0.521 m) (P §46 [p. 72]). W: 56 slots, 28 per side, and 25 niches cut in each wall above them.
- **Floor at the S wall ("virtual end"):** +1689.0 ± 0.5 in (42.901 m), 61.7 ± 0.8 in (1.567 m) S of centre, mid 284.4 ± 2.8 in (7.224 m) E (P §45 [p. 72]).
- **Great step at the top:** its face is 0.4 ± 0.8 in S of the centre (P §46). Face height between the ramps 34.92-35.0 in (E) to 35.80-35.85 in (W) (0.887-0.911 m); length N-S 61.0 in (E) to 61.5 in (W) (1.549-1.562 m); top W side about 1.0 in higher than E; step surface at E side of S doorway +1693.2 ± 0.6 in (43.007 m) (P §46 [p. 75]). Ramp tops 12.18-13.20 in below the step.
- **Upper end opening** to Davison's chamber: in the E wall at the top of the S end, 26.3 in wide at top, 26.2 at base, 29.4 high; passage 28½ wide, 32 high, over 20 ft long (P §62 [p. 91]).

## 10. Well shaft

- **Upper mouth** in the W side of the gallery's lower end: opening 21.8-49.0 in horizontally from the N wall of the gallery on the floor; middle 35.4 in (0.899 m) (P §46 [p. 74]). Petrie gives no further dimensions ("rough... utilitarian"), referring to Smyth.
- **Lower mouth** in the W wall of the descending passage at 3,825-3,856 in along it from the original entrance (P §35).
- In the "grotto" (a gravel-filled rock fissure) the shaft is lined with 10 courses of small stones, 7¼-8 in high (P §46).
- Course (W, citing Haase 2004 and Maragioglio & Rinaldi): vertical about 8 m, then angled S for about the same, reaching bedrock about 5.7 m above base; a vertical section to the grotto; through bedrock at about 45° for 26.5 m, then a steeper 9.5 m, then a nearly horizontal 2.6 m to the descending passage. Total drop about 50 m. Section about 28 in (P §30 compares the trial-passage shaft, 28.63 in, with Pyramid "well widths 28.2", mean diff 0.3).
- **Gap:** no measured 3D path (Smyth's survey is the cited source).

### North Face Corridor and Big Void (ScanPyramids)

- **North Face Corridor (NFC)** (SP23): horizontal cuboid, best estimates width 2.02 ± 0.06 m, height 2.18 ± 0.17 m, length 9.06 ± 0.07 m (emulsion analysis); independent CEA gas detectors: width 1.87 ± 0.11 m, height 1.85 ± 0.12 m, length 9.23 ± 0.48 m. Floor about 20 m above ground; N face 0.84 ± 0.05 m behind the N face of the chevron; E-W axis coincides with the centre of the chevron and lies above the descending corridor (shift from the descending corridor -7 ± 4 cm); slope -0.3 ± 1.5°. "North end of the corridor starts at 0.8 m behind the Chevron and ends at 9.1 m southward." Confirmed by endoscope in Feb 2023 (W): ceiling formed by chevrons.
- **Big Void (SP17):** "at least 30 m" long, cross-section similar to the Grand Gallery, above the gallery (abstract). Nagoya University press release: about 60-70 m above ground. Exact geometry not published in the abstract; possibly inclined or several adjacent structures.

## 12. Surroundings (brief)

- **Queens' pyramids** (E side, S end), all second-hand from W articles: G1-a base 49.5 m, original height 30.25 m; G1-b base 50 m, height 30 m, "ten meters south of G1-a"; G1-c base 46.25 m, height 29.6 m (citing Verner 2001). Satellite pyramid G1-d: ruined, found 1991-93. **Gap:** exact positions/offsets not found (Lehner, *Giza Reports* or GPMP maps would have them).
- **Pyramid Temple:** 52.2 m N-S x 40 m E-W (W, citing Arnold 2005); its doorway 168 m E of the centre, 52.5 m from the E casing (D15). Basalt pavement rock bed, see §1.7.
- **Boat pits:** three boat-shaped pits on the E side, two rectangular pits on the S side (one held the 43.6 m Khufu ship, found 1954; the second excavated from 2011) (W). Petrie's trench measurements (N, S and E.N.E. trenches on the E side): axial lengths about 2130 and 2094 in (54.1, 53.2 m) for the N and S trenches (P §29 [p. 47]).
- **Hetepheres I tomb:** 110 m E of the pyramid (W).
- **Causeway:** runs E/ESE down to the valley temple (buried under Nazlet el-Samman) (W). **Gap:** no measured azimuth found; Petrie's E.N.E. trench is at +76° 02' 26" to the E face (P §29) but that is a trench, not the causeway.
- **Enclosure wall: not found** in the sources read.
